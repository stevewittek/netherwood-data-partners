# First pilot: office software and workflow review

Status: proposed, not performed. No practice name, testimonial, results or
clinical data have been published. This pilot should help the office even if
its owner chooses not to participate in a case study.

## The offer

Review how the office uses its existing software, choose one everyday problem,
make a small agreed improvement, and leave clear instructions. The office
already uses Jane; do not manufacture a migration or replace working software
to create a portfolio project. Position this as a practical systems/workflow
review, not a HIPAA certification, penetration test or clinical audit.

Proposed first scope: one location, one owner/manager, one front-desk workflow,
an inventory of the relevant software/devices, one prioritized findings report,
one agreed improvement and a tested handover. Agree hours, any charge, expenses
and support boundaries before starting; this plan is not a published price or SLA.

## Before the visit

The clinic's authorized owner agrees the scope, access and change window. Your
family relationship does not replace that permission. Participation and later
publicity are separate decisions. The office can approve the review and decline
photos, naming or a case study.

- Identify the owner of the Jane account and the person who can approve changes.
- List what staff want to make easier; choose an actual recurring problem.
- Start with interviews, an empty-office walkthrough and synthetic demonstration
  records. Do not request patient exports just to document the environment.
- Use an individually authorized, appropriately restricted account if access is
  needed. Keep passwords and patient/customer records out of Codex, Git, email
  attachments, Formspark and the public site. If work requires protected health
  information, the clinic must settle appropriate access and business-associate
  arrangements before that access; a public-case-study permission is not enough.
- Have the clinic approve staff participation and media capture separately.

Jane distinguishes staff permissions from Account Owner authority; bulk
export/transfer and ownership changes require the appropriate owner action.
Use the vendor's supported process rather than a shared administrator password.
[Jane staff access guidance](https://jane.app/guide/staff-access-levels).

## Session 1: understand the work

Proposed agenda: 20-minute owner interview, 40-minute staff walkthrough, then
20 minutes to agree the findings and the one improvement. Adjust to the office's
availability; these are planning estimates.

Ask: What task is frustrating? How often does it happen? What do staff repeat or
work around? What happens when the system is unavailable? Who calls the vendor?
What would a visibly better result look like to the person doing the task?

Use this private inventory with non-sensitive identifiers:

| Area | Capture | Proof / question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business task | Booking, intake, front-desk handoff, reporting or another selected workflow | Demonstrate with synthetic data; identify the actual pain point |
| Application | Product, plan, enabled settings and support owner | For hosted Jane, review configuration and workflow; do not claim access to or responsibility for upgrading Jane's servers/database |
| Devices | Device type, OS/browser version and vendor-supported status | Record on the client's protected inventory; no public serial numbers or network map |
| Data flow | Where information is entered, duplicated, exported and reconciled | Map categories and counts, not identifiable patient records |
| Access | Account ownership, roles, departed-user process and recovery owner | Observe approved settings; do not expand permissions for convenience |
| Continuity | Internet/device outage process, vendor contacts, export/recovery responsibilities | Distinguish vendor backup claims from recovery the office can actually perform |
| Reporting | Who uses a report and which total they trust | Have authorized staff validate totals locally; publish only agreed non-identifying measurements |
| Support | Who handles software, devices, network and specialist issues | Agree ownership and escalation; do not assume a formal partner relationship |

## Choose one improvement, not a whole-office overhaul

Possible candidates, only if the review establishes a real problem:

- Make an intake or booking handoff clearer with agreed settings and a short staff guide.
- Remove an unnecessary duplicate-entry step using supported product features.
- Make a routine report repeatable, with an agreed definition and reconciliation check.
- Resolve a supported browser/device problem that disrupts a specific task.
- Document ownership, support and an outage procedure where those are unclear.

If the office is genuinely changing systems later, run a separate migration
scope. Jane supplies an import process; Netherwood's value is preparation,
coordination and acceptance. Confirm supported fields and the changeover window,
review exports, and check the result with the clinic. Do not promise automatic
transfer of every record type or continued synchronization from the old system.
[Jane import overview](https://jane.app/guide/how-to-import-data-to-jane-overview),
[Jane importing hub](https://jane.app/guide/importing-hub).

## Baseline, change and retest

Use the same defined task and comparable synthetic scenarios before and after.
Aim for five trials per condition if practical; record sample size, operator,
date, exceptions and whether results came from a demonstration or actual routine
work. Do not turn one good demonstration into a claim about the whole clinic.

| Metric | Before | After | Measurement / acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active staff time per selected task | Not measured | Not measured | Median timed task, same start/end definition |
| Manual re-entry steps | Not measured | Not measured | Count repeated entry of the same information |
| Errors or rework in tested scenarios | Not measured | Not measured | Record scenarios and observed errors, including failures |
| Documentation coverage | Not reviewed | Not reviewed | Can another authorized staff member follow the instructions? |
| Staff acceptance | Not obtained | Not obtained | Named approver signs off privately; public quote needs separate permission |

Before a change, record the relevant settings/current behavior, the reason for
the change, rollback steps and the agreed test. Use vendor-supported demo/test
facilities where available. Do not send patient reminders, charge cards, create
real appointments or alter clinical records as an improvised smoke test.

After the change: repeat the task, inspect unintended effects, get staff
acceptance, and agree a short follow-up review. Estimate monthly time only if
the clinic supplies a credible task frequency; label it an estimate and show
the calculation. Do not invent savings, revenue gains or compliance outcomes.

## Deliverables the owner can use

1. A one-page owner summary: problem, reviewed scope, top findings, what changed,
   measured result, remaining risks and the next three actions.
2. A private systems/data-flow inventory and prioritized findings register.
3. A change log with evidence, test results and rollback instructions.
4. A concise staff procedure and support/escalation sheet.
5. A signed acceptance record; separately, a permission decision for any public story.

## Turn earned evidence into a case study

The most persuasive story is one recognizable problem, one careful improvement,
and proof that the staff can use it. A photo of equipment alone proves little.

Draft structure, with placeholders that must be replaced by real evidence:

> **Making [specific office task] easier for a small practice**
>
> **Situation:** [what the office was trying to do and the observed friction].
> **Review:** [what Steven checked and what was outside scope].
> **Change:** [the supported change, testing and handover].
> **Result:** [measured before/after, sample size and limitations].
> **Owner's view:** [optional, exact approved quote].
> **Next step:** [the remaining work, rather than a claim that everything is fixed].

Obtain approval of the final text, photos, naming and quote. Explain the family
connection and whether this was a complimentary pilot; do not present it as an
independent paying-client endorsement. An anonymous case can work if specific,
truthful and approved. Do not publish a logo or claim an official Jane or Online
Computers partnership without separate agreement.

## Photo and short-video plan

- Prefer an empty office outside patient hours, Steven explaining the workflow,
  a non-sensitive diagram, and a synthetic/demo screen walkthrough.
- Keep charts, names, calendars, labels, voices and background screens out of
  the shot. Obtain staff permission. Avoid patient footage entirely for this pilot.
- Do not rely on filming first and blurring later. HHS says masking identities
  afterward does not replace the authorization required for media access to
  protected patient information. [HHS filming guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2023/film-and-media/index.html).
- Suggested 60–90 second video: explain the task (15 seconds), show the safe
  demonstration (30 seconds), describe the verified result and limits (20 seconds),
  invite similar business owners to describe their software problem (10 seconds).

## First concrete step

Ask the clinic owner for a short, voluntary discovery visit and one problem the
front desk would like solved. The first approved deliverable is the review and
findings, not a promised success story. No clinic account access, audit, migration,
media capture or publishing has been performed by preparing this plan.
