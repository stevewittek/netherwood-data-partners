Status: working launch checklist; no filings, purchases, accounts, or registrations were made by this implementation. Owner: founder. Official sources checked September 25, 2026. Recheck fees, eligibility, tax treatment, and deadlines when acting. Record completion evidence privately, outside source control.

This document supplements [BUSINESS_READINESS.md](BUSINESS_READINESS.md), [BUSINESS_OPERATING_RUNBOOK.md](BUSINESS_OPERATING_RUNBOOK.md), and [CLIENT_WORK_TEMPLATES.md](CLIENT_WORK_TEMPLATES.md). Their protected operating kit remains the location for completed owner decisions and client records; do not populate those records in Git. Existing readiness documents describe working site/mail services as of September 19, 2026, but do not prove entity formation, revenue, insurance, or bulk-marketing authorization today. Confirm existing records before creating duplicate accounts or changing DNS.

The immediate business objective is to be able to quote, contract, deliver a bounded migration assessment, and collect payment. Complete the items below in priority order; do not wait for a Microsoft designation to sell vendor-neutral services.

## 1. Confirm the legal business and registration position

- [ ] Confirm whether Netherwood already has an entity, EIN, NJ registration, or alternate-name registration. Locate existing records before creating duplicates.
- [ ] Choose the legal structure with an accountant/attorney; an NJ LLC is a candidate, not an assumed completed decision. Record ownership, management, and tax classification choices.
- [ ] Check the intended legal name in the official [NJ business name search](https://www.njportal.com/DOR/BusinessNameSearch/Search/Availability). Domain availability is not legal name availability or trademark clearance.
- [ ] Decide the registered agent, registered office, operating address, and public mailing address. Confirm requirements and municipal home-business rules for the actual address; do not use a mailbox as a fictitious operating location.
- [ ] Verify the live [NJ registration instructions](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/revenue/gettingregistered.shtml), then complete the applicable formation and registration steps. Save the filed certificate and official entity number. The live [NJ fee schedule](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/revenue/fees.shtml), updated July 1, 2026, lists **$100 for an LLC certificate of formation** and **$75 for its annual report**. Confirm the portal total and any convenience charge before paying; older $125 formation references may be stale.
- [ ] Obtain or confirm the EIN directly through the [IRS EIN service](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). The IRS charges no EIN application fee. Use the correct responsible party and legal entity information; retain the confirmation securely. Reconcile the NJ portal sequence with the IRS requirements for the selected entity before filing.
- [ ] Complete NJ-REG for applicable business taxes/employer registration. [NJ Taxation](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/br1.shtml) says to register at least **15 business days before doing business**. If activity has already begun, confirm corrective steps with NJ/your accountant promptly rather than inventing dates. Confirm taxability of each actual service and software/license sale; do not assume all consulting is taxable or exempt.
- [ ] Confirm whether an alternate business name filing is needed when marketing as Netherwood Data Partners. Use the correct legal name and any registered alternate name on contracts, invoices, and banking records.
- [ ] Calendar the LLC annual report: [NJ guidance](https://business.nj.gov/pages/filings-and-accounting) identifies the last day of the formation anniversary month as the due date. Verify the first due year and record registered-agent/address changes.
- [ ] Ask the accountant to verify any current federal ownership-reporting obligations directly with [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/boi), plus applicable federal/state estimated taxes and employer filings. This checklist does not determine applicability or prescribe a BOI filing.

## 2. Make the first engagement easy to buy and pay for

- [ ] Open business checking in the correct legal name; keep business and personal funds separate.
- [ ] Select a business debit/credit card, bookkeeping system, expense-receipt process, and monthly reconciliation owner.
- [ ] Establish invoicing numbers, legal-name/address details, payment instructions, due dates, deposit/milestone policy, and a payment processor if useful. Compare actual fees before choosing one.
- [ ] Arrange a tax-preparation/accounting contact. Confirm record retention and service tax treatment before the first invoice.
- [ ] Turn the **Legacy Systems & Migration Assessment** in [SERVICES.md](SERVICES.md) into the first fixed-scope proposal. Agree the price and delivery date with the customer; no public price or turnaround is promised in these drafts.
- [ ] Use a brief intake: old system, replacement platform, available exports, deadline, data owner, required history, and vendor contact. Keep sensitive data out of the marketing list.

## 3. Insurance and delivery readiness

- [ ] Request quotes for general liability, professional liability/errors & omissions, and cyber liability. Ask a licensed broker which limits, exclusions, retroactive dates, subcontractor coverage, and customer contract requirements fit migration work.
- [ ] Confirm whether any insurance is required by the actual contract, employee situation, or applicable law. This document does not claim that any named policy is universally required.
- [ ] Establish encrypted file exchange, least-privilege access, MFA, backup/restore verification, client-approved retention, and an incident contact before receiving production data.
- [ ] For healthcare, legal, accounting, or other sensitive environments, assess actual data access and contractual obligations before receiving records; obtain specialist advice where needed. Do not label Netherwood compliant or certified without evidence.

## 4. Contracting package for professional review

Have counsel review a practical package, rather than treating this checklist as legal drafting:

| Document or clause | Decision it must make clear |
| --- | --- |
| Master Services Agreement | Parties, responsibilities, confidentiality, liability, termination, dispute process |
| Statement of Work | Systems, data scope, deliverables, exclusions, timetable, dependencies, price |
| NDA | What information may be shared, with whom, and for what purpose |
| Data/security provisions | Access, transfer, storage, subprocessors, incident handling, deletion/return |
| Change order | Who approves scope, fee, or schedule changes before extra work |
| Acceptance criteria | Reconciliation evidence, business tests, signoff owner, review period |
| Payment terms | Deposit, milestones, due date, expenses, licensing responsibility |
| Migration responsibility matrix | Who extracts, maps, configures destination, tests, approves, and cuts over |
| Backup/rollback acknowledgment | Verified backup, recovery decision, rollback trigger, owner, and cutoff |

- [ ] Confirm truthful headers/subjects, clear commercial identity, and opt-out handling. The [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) applies to B2B commercial email, requires an opt-out mechanism available for at least 30 days after sending, and requires opt-outs honored within 10 business days. Netherwood should suppress immediately.
- [ ] Explicitly approve the final audience, message, sender, landing page, and send count. Importing records is never campaign approval.

## 6. First customer acquisition cycle

1. Confirm Revival's actual scope and any useful next paid engagement without inventing project results.
2. Finalize one assessment offer, one landing page, and one way to request a conversation.
3. Assemble one small permitted list for a specific county/industry or obtain referrals; keep provenance and opt-out history.
4. Preview and test, then have the founder explicitly authorize any real send in the production workflow.
5. Record inquiries, booked conversations, and customers against the campaign. Review weekly and improve the offer or audience using actual results.

Success at launch means a real prospect can understand the offer, contact Netherwood, receive a credible proposal, sign an appropriate agreement, and pay a correct invoice. Filing completion and public claims require their own evidence.
